Introduction to Psychological Research

Evidence, Scientific Method, and Critical Thinking in Behavioral Science

5-Min Warm-Up

How do we know why people behave the way they do?

Is intuition enough to understand human action? Throughout history, people relied on folk wisdom, personal anecdotes, and gut feelings. Modern psychology uses empirical evidence to differentiate objective psychological reality from subjective belief.

1. Evidence vs. Opinion

Opinion: A subjective belief or preference not necessarily grounded in measurable facts.
Observation: Directly recording behavior or events without drawing unverified conclusions.
Evidence-Based Claim: An assertion backed by systematic, empirical data gathered through controlled research.

Scientific Rigor Meter
High Empirical Rigor

2. Systematic Empirical Method

Human perception is susceptible to biases like **Confirmation Bias** and **Hindsight Bias**. Systematic research applies standardized operational definitions, controls variables, and replicates results to minimize error and subjective distortion.

Bias Reduction Score
90% Bias Control

3. Critical Thinking

Critical thinkers constantly ask: *What is the evidence? Is the sample representative? Are there alternative explanations?* Critical thinking requires skepticism combined with openness to peer-reviewed data.

Critical Analysis Level
Advanced B2 Logic
